# qatar-abtest

Plans and reads A/B tests run in Qatar: sample size, z and Welch tests with intervals for the difference and the lift, a bootstrap for revenue in QAR, CUPED, a Bayesian read, Holm and BH corrections, a sample ratio check, O'Brien-Fleming boundaries for looking early, and warnings when Ramadan, Eid or a public holiday lands unevenly in the test window. It also compares two models scored on the same test cases.

```
pip install .
```

Python 3.10 or newer. statsmodels is only needed for the tests (`pip install .[dev]`).

## Planning

A grocery app in Doha converts 12.7% of visitors and gets about 600 new users a day:

```
$ qabtest size --baseline 0.127 --lift 0.10 --daily-users 600
conversion 12.70% -> 13.97% (+10.0%), alpha 0.05, power 80%
users per group: 11,242 control, 11,242 treatment (22,484 in total)
at 600 users a day: 38 days, so run 6 whole weeks (42 days)
```

For revenue per user, give the standard deviation and the change in QAR. `--cuped-r` is the correlation with the same metric before the test; CUPED multiplies the variance by 1 - r^2:

```
$ qabtest size --sd 120 --mde 5 --cuped-r 0.28 --daily-users 600
mean difference 5 with sd 120 (d = 0.042), alpha 0.05, power 80%
users per group: 9,043 control, 9,043 treatment (18,086 in total)
with CUPED (pre-period r = 0.28): 8,334 and 8,334 (16,668, -8%)
at 600 users a day: 31 days, so run 5 whole weeks (35 days)
at 600 users a day with CUPED: 28 days, so run 4 whole weeks (28 days)
```

Durations are rounded up to whole weeks because the Friday and Saturday weekend shops differently from the rest of the week.

The QAR 100 threshold lifts conversion past the look 2 bound, but its revenue gain is not clear yet, and half the window is Ramadan while the January pre-period has none of it. When a result has p below 0.05 but stays inside the bound, the output says that stopping would be peeking.

The input is one row per user. `--converted` is a 0/1 column, `--revenue` any numeric metric, `--pre` the same metric before the test. `--expected 50,25,25` sets an unequal planned split (control first). `--correct` is `holm` (default), `bh` or `none`, applied to the conversion and revenue tests of every variant (the CUPED revenue test when `--pre` is given). `--json -` prints everything as JSON.

## The Qatar calendar

Ramadan and the two Eids come from the Umm al-Qura calendar through `hijridate`. Qatar fixes Ramadan and Eid by moon sighting, so the real dates can differ by a day from the calculated ones, and the announced Eid holiday is often longer than the days used here: 1 to 3 Shawwal for Eid al-Fitr and 9 to 12 Dhu al-Hijjah for Arafat Day and Eid al-Adha. National Day (18 December) and National Sport Day (second Tuesday of February) are fixed. The weekend is Friday and Saturday.

```
$ qabtest calendar 2026-03-08 2026-03-28 --pre-period 2026-02-01 2026-02-21
the pre-period: 2026-02-01 to 2026-02-21, 21 days, 6 on Friday or Saturday, 4 in Ramadan; National Sport Day 2026-02-10
the test: 2026-03-08 to 2026-03-28, 21 days, 6 on Friday or Saturday, 12 in Ramadan; Eid al-Fitr 2026-03-20 to 2026-03-22
  ! Ramadan covers 12 of 21 days of the test (57%), so the result mixes Ramadan and ordinary days
  ! Eid al-Fitr falls in the test (2026-03-20 to 2026-03-22)
  ! Ramadan days: 100% of the first half against 18% of the second half
  ! Eid and holiday days: 0 in the first half against 3 in the second half
  ! Ramadan days: 19% of the pre-period against 57% of the test
```

It warns about windows that are not whole weeks, Ramadan covering part of the window, any Eid or holiday inside it, and Ramadan or holiday shares that differ by 10 points or more between the two halves of the test or between the pre-period and the test.

## Looking early

Checking a test every few days and stopping at the first p < 0.05 inflates false positives: 8.3% with two looks, 14.2% with five. `qabtest boundaries` gives Lan-DeMets spending bounds of O'Brien-Fleming type, which keep the overall rate at alpha:

```
$ qabtest boundaries --looks 4
O'Brien-Fleming type alpha spending (Lan-DeMets), two-sided alpha 0.05

look  share of sample  bound |z|  nominal p  alpha spent
   1              25%      4.333    0.00001      0.00001
   2              50%      2.963    0.00305      0.00305
   3              75%      2.359    0.01832      0.01930
   4             100%      2.014    0.04400      0.05000

stopping at the first p < 0.05 over 4 looks instead: false positive rate 0.126
```

`--fractions 0.3,0.6,1` sets unequal looks. A test simulates 20,000 A/A tests with five looks each: naive peeking gives about 14% false positives, the bounds about 5%.

## Comparing two models

`qabtest compare` reads per-case files (CSV or JSON Lines, an `id` column and a score column) and compares each system with the first one on the cases they share. It prints a 95% paired bootstrap interval for the mean difference and a sign-flip permutation p-value (exact up to 16 non-zero differences), adjusted across systems. When every score is 0 or 1 it adds McNemar's test.

It is used on the retrieval evaluation of [qanun](https://github.com/Ifazawezk/qanun). `examples/qanun/export_per_case.py` turns qanun's `eval/results.json` into one file per method, with recall@k and MRR@10 per question as qanun defines them:

```
$ qabtest compare examples/qanun/cross-language-translation.jsonl \
    examples/qanun/cross-language-translation-mmarco.jsonl examples/qanun/cross-language-mmarco.jsonl --metric hit@1
hit@1 on 60 paired cases, baseline cross-language-translation (0.467)
95% paired bootstrap interval (10000 resamples, seed 0); sign-flip permutation p, adjusted with holm

cross-language-translation-mmarco  0.650  diff +0.183 (+0.067 to +0.300)  p 0.0074  adjusted 0.0148
                                   McNemar: 2 cases only cross-language-translation gets right, 13 only cross-language-translation-mmarco; p 0.0074 (exact)
cross-language-mmarco              0.567  diff +0.100 (+0.000 to +0.217)  p 0.1460  adjusted 0.1460
                                   McNemar: 3 cases only cross-language-translation gets right, 9 only cross-language-mmarco; p 0.1460 (exact)
```

With the default 10,000 resamples and seed 0 the bootstrap draws the same resamples as qanun, and `tests/test_qanun_example.py` checks all 45 intervals in qanun's `eval/results.md` (recall@4, recall@5 and MRR@10 for 15 comparisons). All of them match to three decimals.

ACAS's `tools/evaluate_analyzer.py` prints totals only. Once it writes one row per comment with a 0/1 `correct` column, `qabtest compare old.csv new.csv --metric correct` runs McNemar on the old and new analyzer.

## Exit codes

| Code | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | done; for `analyze`, the sample ratio is fine; for `calendar`, no warnings |
| 1 | `analyze` found a sample ratio mismatch (p < 0.001), or `calendar` printed a warning |
| 2 | bad arguments, a missing file or column, or values the methods cannot use |

## How the numbers are checked

Every formula has a test against a reference value:

- Sample sizes match statsmodels' `NormalIndPower` and `TTestIndPower`, and Cohen (1988): 392 per group for h = 0.2 and 64 for d = 0.5 at 80% power.
- The z test, the Wald interval for the difference and the Katz interval for the lift match statsmodels' `proportions_ztest` and `confint_proportions_2indep`; Welch matches scipy and statsmodels; the bootstrap matches `scipy.stats.bootstrap` within resampling noise.
- CUPED reduces variance by r^2 on simulated data, as in Deng, Xu, Kohavi and Walker (2013).
- P(B beats A) uses Evan Miller's closed form and gives 2/3 for Beta(2, 1) against a uniform; the expected loss for two uniforms is 1/6.
- Holm and BH match statsmodels' `multipletests` and the 15 p-values in Benjamini and Hochberg (1995), where BH rejects 4 and Holm 3.
- Boundaries match the Lan-DeMets O'Brien-Fleming values 4.877, 3.357, 2.680, 2.290, 2.031 for five looks; naive peeking matches Armitage, McPherson and Rowe (1969) as tabled by Jennison and Turnbull (2000).
- McNemar matches statsmodels and Agresti's (2002) approval rating example (z^2 = 17.36); the permutation test matches `scipy.stats.permutation_test`.
- The sample ratio check follows Fabijan et al. (2019) and is checked by hand: 50,000 against 50,600 gives chi2 3.578, p 0.0586.

## Limits

The analysis assumes users are independent and each is counted once; it does not handle ratio metrics such as revenue per order without the delta method on your side. Bayesian results use a uniform Beta(1, 1) prior. The boundaries assume the planned number of looks and roughly equal information between them, and they are two-sided. Calendar dates are calculated, so check the official announcement for Ramadan and Eid. Example data in `examples/checkout_test.csv` is generated; the qanun files are derived from qanun's published evaluation.
